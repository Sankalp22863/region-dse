## One query buys one point

High-level synthesis lets you write a kernel in C and get a circuit out the other end. What it does not do is decide how that circuit should be *shaped*. That is set by a handful of transformation knobs: how much to unroll each loop, what order to nest them in, how to tile for locality, how to partition arrays so the datapath can actually be fed.

Those knobs interact, and there are many of them. Depending on the kernel, the legal configuration space runs from about 10^4 to 10^8 points. Worse, the space is opaque: the only way to know what a configuration really costs — cycles, DSPs, LUTs, flip-flops, block RAM — is to synthesize it, and that takes minutes to hours per query.

So autotuners face a brutal ratio: an enormous space against a budget of a few hundred measurements. And every existing approach spends that budget the same way — pick a point, measure it, use the result to pick the next.

> **One query buys one point of information.** Nothing is amortized. A neighbor that has already revealed the region as hopeless still costs a fresh evaluation.
